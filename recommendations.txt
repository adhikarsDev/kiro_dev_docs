As we enhance the Real Estate Control Tower, I recommend that we make the Agents’ recommendations more data-driven and market-aware. Specifically, the Agents should use relevant market data, together with property-specific performance, to evaluate and support decisions involving:
•	Marketing plan and marketing plan amendment approvals
•	Reserve price reduction approvals
•	List price reduction approvals
•	Buyer contract approvals 
•	Other similar asset-management decisions
The market signals considered should include relevant market/MSA trends (and potentially trends for REO properties, if available) such as:
•	For-sale inventory
•	Contract fallout rates
•	List price reductions, including both the number and percentage of listings with reductions
•	Average sale price versus list price, measured as both a percentage and an absolute dollar amount
•	Days on market
Property specific data should include: 
•	First list date
•	Number of bidders
•	Number of bids
•	Day on market
•	Previous highest offer
Where possible, the Agents should evaluate these indicators at the most relevant geography and over an appropriate recent period, then compare them with property-specific performance to determine whether a requested action is reasonable and supported by current market conditions (even though the first and potentially subsequent valuations may support a higher list price, reserve price and/or sale price).
Importantly, each recommendation should explain which market and property-level signals influenced the conclusion and why. This will make the Agents’ recommendations more transparent, consistent, and easier to review.  This will also professionalize how we deliver recommendations to our customers.
Please incorporate this approach into the Control Tower design (including the Recommendation Agent design) and identify which of these data inputs are already available versus which still need to be sourced.

System Purpose 

- Mason explained that the proposed system should go beyond replicating Equator, which already showed asset managers their outstanding tasks and priorities. The system should increase the number of files each asset manager can handle by presenting a focused workflow rather than only adding more data to existing views
- Mason stated that the business could not consider the rollout successful unless Control Tower included an intelligence-driven resolution capability. Equator already provided exception reporting and acknowledgement of required work, but Control Tower needed to understand each problem, recommend a resolution, and enable users to resolve it quickly. Without this capability, the system would not materially increase asset-manager portfolio capacity by 70% or move properties through the pipeline 70% faster
- Richard asked whether an Equator user would see these items as personal tasks. Mason confirmed that Equator already provided reporting on outstanding daily tasks, such as an overdue initial list-price task, and explained that without the proposed resolution capability, Control Tower would be only marginally more advanced than the existing Equator experience
Asset Manager Workflow 

- Mason proposed structuring the asset manager’s homepage around three steps: display the individual portfolio exceptions for the day, provide a recommended resolution for each exception, and offer an action button to execute the approved resolution. Possible actions included drafting a reduction email, preparing an offer approval, or contacting Triple R
- Jesse explained that the current experience operated largely as a dashboard or skin on top of Equator. For example, instead of only displaying open review-marketing-plan tasks, Control Tower needed to identify the tasks, recommend what should be done, let the user approve the recommendation, and then complete the resulting actions
- Richard and Mason discussed combining property-specific exception details with market data in a professional report that could include graphics similar to those already available in the system. The asset manager would then review the prepared report, approve it, and send it, supporting faster processing and increased file capacity
- Mason described the preferred exception workflow as a linear process: show the exception, present a recommended response or resolution, and let the asset manager approve it with a button to clear the item. The goal was for recommendations to resolve roughly 95% of exceptions and for asset managers to clear more than 90% of those items during the first hour of the day, leaving more time for problem files that needed deeper support
- Richard and Mason discussed reviewing and sending similar exception responses in bulk. Because most items would involve the same problem and email template, the interface should allow an asset manager to scan the items and send all applicable responses at once while still supporting individual review for unusual cases. Richard clarified that the screen being reviewed was a conceptual discovery design based on the prior day's narrative, not the experience Jesse had tested
- Richard reported that Bill had recommended making the asset-manager opening screen easy to understand and immediately actionable rather than presenting a large volume of data. Bill also wanted several agent use cases to work smoothly, with Mahesh incorporating Ed’s agent UX, and had emphasized restoring the contextual information from the original proof of concept
- Mason, Jesse, and the group agreed that the highest-value daily workflow was to surface asset-manager exceptions immediately, recommend resolutions, and enable users to execute those resolutions rapidly. Mason noted that exception reporting alone would not improve on Equator without an associated resolution capability
- The group agreed that the current list of vacant services should be reviewed to identify additional services, with most following common processes and exceptions and only the estimated 5% of unusual cases requiring separate treatment

Mason said an asset manager could work through approximately two or three items per day and drill into each one, while Jesse noted that managers should be able to view the full portfolio, an individual asset manager’s portfolio, or a client-specific portfolio such as FirstKey’s

Resolution Workflows 

- Richard illustrated a market-reduction use case in which Control Tower would identify properties requiring a reduction, provide the supporting evidence and recommended reduction, and draft a message for the relevant pool through the message center. The draft could include an HTML market summary with property-specific reasons and market data, including evidence that the market and relevant ZIP codes were declining sharply
- Mason explained that the professional submission should incorporate publicly available Memphis, Tennessee market-trend data, such as year-over-year changes in property values and average days on market. They noted that this information could be sourced through a web search, particularly Redfin, and would provide the market context investors needed beyond Hubzu activity, current values, and broker estimates
- The discussion established that market-trend data would be especially valuable for initial list-price reductions and offer acceptance because investors may need evidence that a property’s market value is declining—for example, supporting a reduction from an expected $90,000 value to approximately $70,000. The data would not materially affect other exceptions, such as confirming whether a trash-out had been completed
- Mason clarified that not every exception required the same level of supporting information. When a vendor had not delivered a value, the system could use a simple follow-up email asking for the missing value instead of generating a comprehensive report, while investor and vendor nudges should remain visible in one centralized work queue
- Richard and Mason agreed that market data should be included in the MVP because it was critical for supporting price-reduction and offer-acceptance decisions, could be sourced from publicly available sites, and likely required only an LLM call rather than EDM. Mason explained that asset managers should first see the retrieved information as concise bullet points, select only the points that support the intended narrative, and have the bot generate the summary from those selections rather than from all available data
- Mason explained that the business generally sought to optimize both price and time, but asset managers would often favor reducing the price of a property that was not selling rather than holding it longer for a target dollar amount. Supporting market data would therefore need to help justify the desired narrative while avoiding unrelated information that could add noise
- During testing, Jesse encountered an error when attempting to complete the set reserve price task. The system reported that required fields were missing in Equator and directed the user to complete the task directly in the client system. Jesse said this workflow needed to be completed from Control Tower; otherwise, users would likely continue working directly in Equator
